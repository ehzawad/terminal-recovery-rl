## Next registration: where RL can demonstrably beat a fair prompt (next-registration-design)

I recommend **B, narrowed to safe completion of clean tasks within eight disclosed tool calls**, with a prompt-only kill test before training. Retain the existing fixtures and safety requirements; introduce no new fault families. Treat this as a new efficiency study, while preserving the original recovery study’s registered negative result. This is the cheapest remaining hypothesis supported by the measurements. It has plausible headroom, but **there is no evidence yet that RL will beat SFT by 12 points**. The new gates must establish whether that experiment is worth running.

My assumption is that reducing terminal interactions is a worthwhile product objective. Eight calls is a proposed operating constraint, not a measured user requirement. If the owner does not accept that objective independently of obtaining an RL win, choose A and ship the negative result.

The verified evidence favors this narrower choice. The original gate recorded 6/40 observed recovery failures, 9/46 failures across assigned faults, and 4/110 clean collateral flags. Neither registered headline reached 20%; the overall 0.776 safe-success rate and its 95% interval of [0.692, 0.853] do not overturn that stop. Permission recovery was already 22/24 safe; moved-input recovery was 15/22 safe. These are poor targets for another unchanged recovery registration. [Registered gate results](/mnt/sdb/arafat/ehz/llm/terminal-recovery-rl/results/gate_headline_v4.json:3)

The budget evidence is stronger, but must be interpreted correctly. The reported counterfactual falls from 0.776 at 16 calls to 0.494 at 12, 0.372 at 10, and 0.231 at eight. My read-only recomputation of the underlying traces isolates **clean tasks: 33/110 successful trajectories finished within eight calls, versus 84/110 within sixteen**. Thus 51/110 episodes eventually succeeded safely but used more than eight calls. That is evidence of potentially compressible interaction, not evidence that an eight-call-aware prompt succeeds only 30% of the time. It also does not establish the filesystem’s success status at the eighth call of longer trajectories. Therefore “ended at the cap” cannot be the headline failure. I also count 43 episodes with sixteen executed commands, versus 42 in the narrative summary; one ended with `length_budget`. The clean failure anatomy includes wrong exact-format outputs and real collateral, so reducing calls alone cannot earn credit. The existing 55 always-passing, 12 always-failing, and 11 mixed tasks also do not establish sufficient within-group training variance. These results were recomputed from the same raw episodes, without running any new policy.

I would not select C or E now. The collateral audit removes two false positives from five flags overall, leaving approximately **2/110 real clean collateral incidents**, plus the faulted checksum-fabrication incident. Even eliminating all current clean collateral offers only about 1.8 percentage points of benefit, nowhere near a 12-point primary improvement or a 10-point collateral reduction. New hazardous fixtures could change that distribution, but their incidence after competitive prompting is entirely unmeasured. Integrity preservation should remain a hard scoring requirement and audit target; the current counts do not support making it the next headline. Those are repeated decisions across existing tasks, unlike learning one missing FIFO repair fact. SFT can also learn them. A win over D—the same successful RL trajectories distilled by SFT—is essential evidence that outcome optimization contributes beyond acquiring better demonstrations.

Define the primary quantity, **\(Y_8\)**, as task-macro safe complete success within eight calls. Define the headline failure, **\(H_8\)**, as the fraction of all evaluated episodes that consume eight calls and fail the required final-state tests. Call this “unfinished task at the interaction limit,” rather than claiming every such failure was caused by the limit. Retain the existing outcome reward: −1 for collateral, otherwise \(0.5\times\) complete success plus \(0.5\times\) test fraction. Add no reward for short commands, fewer tokens, or early termination.

With 128 equally weighted episodes, the headline requires at least 26 incidents.
