## Skeptic: manufactured headroom and unfair controls (headroom-honesty-skeptic)

The stop decision is sound under the registered rule and should remain in force. There are material findings in this lens: the collateral oracle manufactures a small amount of headroom, the FIFO narrative misdescribes the traces, and B–E each permit a misleading positive result unless their controls are strengthened. A is defensible now, provided it reports a stopped study rather than evidence that RL cannot help. My preferred continuation, if one is pursued, is a revised D with fully informed prompting controls and E as a separately measured integrity outcome.

Verified evidence supports the stop. A4 explicitly gates on task failure **conditional on observing the fault**, alongside collateral on clean episodes; neither reaching 20% requires stopping. Recomputing the existing traces gives 6/40 observed-fault failures, 9/46 all-assigned-fault failures, and four clean collateral flags. I checked row uniqueness, correspondence to assigned configurations, and the implemented observation rubric; those checks matched the records. The six unobserved faulted episodes contain three successes and three failures. Excluding those episodes follows the registration, although it makes 15% an unsuitable standalone measure of general recovery competence. The 19.6% all-assigned rate also remains below 20%; it must not be rounded into passing. See [A4 and A5](/mnt/sdb/arafat/ehz/llm/terminal-recovery-rl/PREREGISTRATION.md:163) and [gate calculation](/mnt/sdb/arafat/ehz/llm/terminal-recovery-rl/scripts/gates.py:54).

The collateral false positives are verified, not merely plausible. The migration instruction explicitly requires moving each directory and then creating its replacement symlink. Its contract permits `type_changed` at the original directory paths. The oracle reports an absent intermediate path as `deleted`, and the environment permanently latches that event. Both affected traces execute legitimate moves followed by symlinks and pass all nine final tests. A direct call to the existing oracle with the actual contract reproduces rejection of the intermediate absence and acceptance of the completed symlink. The evidence is in the [contract](/mnt/sdb/arafat/ehz/llm/terminal-recovery-rl/data/contracts_v4_frozen.jsonl:178), [first affected trace](/mnt/sdb/arafat/ehz/llm/terminal-recovery-rl/runs/gates_v4/headline_P.jsonl:38), [second affected trace](/mnt/sdb/arafat/ehz/llm/terminal-recovery-rl/runs/gates_v4/headline_P.jsonl:68), [change classifier](/mnt/sdb/arafat/ehz/llm/terminal-recovery-rl/termrl/manifest.py:41), and [latching logic](/mnt/sdb/arafat/ehz/llm/terminal-recovery-rl/termrl/env.py:177).

Correcting just these two documented false positives changes clean collateral from 4/110 to **2/110 = 1.82%** and safe success from 121/156 to **123/156 = 78.85%**. Repeating the registered task-cluster bootstrap with its existing seed gives approximately **[70.51%, 86.54%]**. Headroom therefore still passes, and the headline still fails. Recovery incidence is unchanged because both corrected episodes are clean. These are audit corrections to existing observations, not a new model evaluation. They strengthen the stop; they do not reopen it.

There is also a specification mismatch worth fixing before another study. A5 specifies pooled **task failure**, explicitly “not safe success,” for the final all-assigned mechanism. Both the gate’s all-assigned diagnostic and final analysis instead use `not safe_success`. That conflates collateral with task failure. It happens to produce the same 9/46 today because every successful faulted episode is also safe; it could change a future claimed mechanism improvement. Report task failure and unsafe-or-failed completion separately, with an explicit error policy. See [registered mechanism](/mnt/sdb/arafat/ehz/llm/terminal-recovery-rl/PREREGISTRATION.md:206), [gate implementation](/mnt/sdb/arafat/ehz/llm/terminal-recovery-rl/scripts/gates.py:59), and [final analysis implementation](/mnt/sdb/arafat/ehz/llm/terminal-recovery-rl/scripts/analyze.py:121).

The FIFO aggregate results also reproduce: 20/59 safe successes and 33/52 observed-fault failures. However, the explanation “it never names the FIFO / pipe type and never restores the input” is contradicted by the traces. **46/59 episodes mention “FIFO” or “named pipe” in assistant content; 10/59 record the fault cleared, including six successful episodes.** One successful trace explicitly diagnoses the pipe and replaces it with the backup. Conversely, **14 of the 20 successful episodes leave the fault uncleared**. The grader restores intact FIFO backups before testing, so merely failing to restore the input does not necessarily explain task failure. Recognition, repair, and task completion are distinct outcomes here. See the [successful repair trace](/mnt/sdb/arafat/ehz/llm/terminal-recovery-rl/runs/probe_fifo_v4/P.jsonl:6), [diagnosis followed by failure](/mnt/sdb/arafat/ehz/llm/terminal-recovery-rl/runs/probe_fifo_v4/P.jsonl:29), and [grading restoration](/mnt/sdb/arafat/ehz/llm/terminal-recovery-rl/termrl/env.py:210). The inference that prompt naming *causes* the difference between permission/move and FIFO performance is consequently unproven; those families also differ in difficulty and timeout behavior.

These qualifications affect the conclusion’s wording. The evidence supports “the registered development gate found insufficient incidence of the selected failures to proceed.” It does not establish a population recovery-failure rate below 20%, establish that prompting generally suffices, or demonstrate that RL would fail. No RL comparison occurred. FIFO supplies exploratory evidence against broad robustness, but it was a secondary held-out family, not an alternative gate that can replace the stopped gate after inspection.

The oracle **must be fixed before any new scored gate**, including a budget study. Otherwise RL could improve its reward merely by putting `mv` and `ln -s` inside one tool call, eliminating an observation boundary without improving the underlying operation. The validation procedure explains why this escaped: it runs the reference script as one command and inspects changes afterward, whereas the model’s equivalent sequence crosses multiple boundaries. See [reference validation](/mnt/sdb/arafat/ehz/llm/terminal-recovery-rl/scripts/validate_tasks.py:45).

A credible repair needs explicit permitted transitions. For a registered directory-to-symlink migration, temporary absence at the original path should be accepted when the original data remains intact at the authorized destination; final grading must require the correct replacement link and preserved data. Do not globally equate `type_changed` permission with permission to delete permanently. Preserve independent checks for unauthorized mode, owner, content, and unrelated-path changes. Validate equivalent combined-call and split-call executions, plus negative cases covering lost data, wrong destinations, missing final links, and unrelated damage. Version the semantics and contracts, revalidate affected cases, and apply the corrected scorer to every arm before collecting new gate data. Preserve the original result and append its correction.

For **A**, there is no positive RL result to assess. Shipping the harness and stopped study is credible if the artifact clearly distinguishes historical scores, corrected scores, and exploratory FIFO results. Calling it a negative *RL comparison* would be inaccurate. Its strongest supporting control is reproducible scoring plus independent audits of accepted alternative solutions and oracle errors; further training is unnecessary to substantiate the historical stop. Nothing later falsifies the fact that the registered gate stopped, but a corrected calculation crossing its threshold would falsify the claim that the stop followed that gate. That did not happen. A later preregistered RL win could falsify a broader “RL offers no benefit” interpretation, which A should never make.

For **B**, choosing eight calls because the observed completion-length curve falls to 23% is manufactured headroom unless the constraint has an independent justification. Eight calls can define a legitimate efficiency benchmark, but it does not automatically represent the owner’s deployment problem. The existing curve is also not an experiment with an eight-call-aware policy: it counts successful existing trajectories whose total command count is at most eight. It does not determine whether longer trajectories already had a correct filesystem at call eight, or how warning the model would change its behavior.

The budget evidence is weaker than its shorthand suggests. Fourteen of the 33 episodes ending at `max_tool_turns` already have safe, successful final states under the original scorer. Thus “ended at cap” is not “failed because of cap.” I also count 43 traces executing all 16 commands, rather than the reported 42; that discrepancy is minor, but the outcome distinction is material. The rollout grades the final state even when another requested tool turn is denied. See [budget handling](/mnt/sdb/arafat/ehz/llm/terminal-recovery-rl/termrl/rollout.py:129).

A positive B result becomes credible as **competence under a specified interaction budget** if P is reselected with the exact budget disclosed, explicit batching advice, and competitive examples or reusable command patterns derived from training data. Every arm needs identical token, context, timeout, tool, and budget-counter rules. Freeze the budget before new outcomes, evaluate unseen tasks at that budget and adjacent budgets, and report wall time and tokens alongside calls. A gain that disappears against budget-aware P or S/D falsifies the RL-specific claim. A gain confined to the selected cutoff does not necessarily falsify a narrow eight-call result, but undermines general efficiency claims; increased latency or tokens falsifies those corresponding efficiency claims. A “safety” improvement produced solely by fewer inspection boundaries is an oracle artifact. Independent auditing of actions within batched commands is especially important here.

For **C**, adversarial fixtures are legitimate for a stated stress test. They manufacture misleading headroom when selected because a particular prompt fails, when protection is defined by a hidden label, or when the resulting incidence is presented as ordinary user prevalence. A planted `.log` file is not a protected decoy if the user requested deleting every `.log` file. Dotfiles, symlinks, and read-only files likewise need task-specific scope rules; their properties alone do not establish that touching them violates intent. The existing fixture generator deliberately avoids mentioned names and extensions, so moving to overlapping decoys changes this validity problem materially. See [fixture assumptions](/mnt/sdb/arafat/ehz/llm/terminal-recovery-rl/termrl/fixtures.py:1).

A credible C comparison gives every arm the same clear scope and preservation rules, lets P use explicit safe-glob, quoting, traversal, and mode-preservation guidance, and tests independently authored fixtures whose protected status can be determined from the instruction and observable environment. Hold out task templates, fixture constructions, and path/content patterns; include ordinary cases without traps and cases where broad operations are actually authorized. Measure safe task completion so abstaining or processing nothing cannot win. Validate alternative correct solutions, not just the reference. Gains that vanish under informed P, fail on new fixture constructions, or arise through incomplete execution falsify the proposed RL advantage. Success only on a deliberately enriched hazard distribution supports that distribution’s result, not a claim about everyday collateral frequency. Symlink destinations outside `/home/user` also require expanded measurement coverage before they can support an outcome; that region is currently excluded from the stated oracle scope.

For **D**, “families the prompt does not name” is not a sufficient definition of generalization. An arbitrary ban on supplying relevant Linux troubleshooting knowledge weakens P. Conversely, holding out actual training examples from a family is legitimate, even though the instruct model may already know the concept. The key distinction is between withholding evaluation instances and withholding useful general instructions from the control.

A credible revised D should include an explicitly informed prompting control: file-type checks, bounded reads, backup discovery, preservation of authoritative inputs, and the documented repair permissions. Where the family catalogue is known, a runbook that names those families without revealing the current injected instance is a useful strong control. A stricter family-transfer comparison can additionally freeze all arms without held-out-family examples or family-specific tuning. Freeze a multi-family evaluation suite and its weights before seeing its outcomes; include matched clean tasks, repairable cases, and a separately defined outcome for genuinely unrecoverable cases. Report task completion, fault clearance before grader repair, and integrity independently. Mid-episode faults need a reproducible injection rule that does not selectively penalize one policy’s inspection strategy.

D’s positive result is credible if RL beats informed P and S/D on genuinely held-out tasks and mechanisms while retaining clean performance. If a short informed prompt closes the gap, the claim of advantage over competitive prompting is falsified, even if RL beats the original p6. If gains depend on `.bak` naming, transfer poorly beyond FIFO, or disappear when grader assistance is separated out, the broader recovery claim fails. FIFO can remain held out from **training**, but it is now a known development weakness; it cannot honestly serve as an untouched discovery about an unknown family in the next registration.

For **E**, the checksum incident is a meaningful behavioral failure: the model finds and reads the authoritative sidecar, then regenerates another sidecar from the data it is supposed to verify. But one event does not establish sufficient incidence for a dedicated headline. The existing fault-integrity oracle already detects this example, so adding a detector alone does not create a new scientific question. Its failure is also partly manifested as a formatting difference in the regenerated sidecar; learning the original filename format could avoid the hash-based detector without learning the intended integrity principle. See the [checksum trace](/mnt/sdb/arafat/ehz/llm/terminal-recovery-rl/runs/gates_v4/headline_P.jsonl:133).

A credible E study makes authoritative inputs, editable outputs, allowed restoration, and verification requirements explicit to every arm, with a strong “preserve reference artifacts; restore from originals; do not regenerate expected evidence” prompt. Evaluate recoverable references, genuinely corrupted data, and missing references where the correct response cannot be an unsupported verification claim. Include tasks that legitimately request generating new checksums, so the model cannot succeed by refusing all checksum creation. Hold out reference formats, artifact types, and recovery locations. Validate the detector against legal restoration and audit unsupported reports as well as altered input files. State whether byte-identical regeneration is in scope and how it is observed; the present manifest cannot establish its provenance. The defensible endpoint is observable integrity violations, not an inference about internal honesty. Gains that disappear against explicit P, depend on detector blind spots, or trade violations for excessive noncompletion falsify the intended claim.

One additional correction matters to C/E’s motivation: the SSL example should not be described as exposing a previously restrictive private directory. Its initial mode is **777**, and recreation changes it to 755. It violates the registered property contract and accompanies serious task failure—including later placeholder certificate/key content—but it is not evidence of permission broadening. See the [SSL trace](/mnt/sdb/arafat/ehz/llm/terminal-recovery-rl/runs/gates_v4/headline_P.jsonl:132). That does not remove its registered flag; it limits the safety story that flag supports.

My assessment depends on a common requirement across B–E: freeze the new construct, task generator, prompt-selection allowance, denominators, thresholds, and held-out evaluation before collecting deciding data. Retain P/S/D comparisons and give controls access to the same general rules and legitimate recovery information. Failure to meet a promotion threshold means the claimed result was not demonstrated; a noisy estimate alone does not prove zero benefit. Repeatedly changing budgets, hazards, or headlines until one passes must be disclosed as adaptive development, with a genuinely untouched final evaluation.

I made no repository changes and ran no new model or sandbox evaluations; the checks above used existing development traces, source inspection, arithmetic, and a small oracle reproduction. The remaining owner decisions are whether a concrete deployment constraint justifies B, whether C is intended as an adversarial stress benchmark or a prevalence claim, and whether D’s product requirement is successful task completion with permitted workarounds or restoration of the environment itself. None is required to preserve today’s stop.
