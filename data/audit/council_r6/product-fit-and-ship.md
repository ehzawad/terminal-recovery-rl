## Owner's product fit and the public artefact (product-fit-and-ship)

**Recommendation: register C, scope-safe execution, as the next pilot, and publish A’s stopped-gate result now.** The product promises to execute an English intent safely. A benchmark that checks whether the command completes the requested work *and* preserves everything outside the authorized scope is the most useful product artefact, even if a strong prompt wins and no adapter is trained. D is the next-best research question because the FIFO probe shows a substantial recovery gap. B measures efficiency; E addresses a serious but currently sparse integrity failure. A is an honest release, but it is not a new RL experiment.

**Verified evidence.** The registered v4.1 gate stopped before S or R: P’s safe success was 0.776, observed recovery failure was 6/40 (15%), and clean collateral flags were 4/110 (3.6%). The gate result records no headline meeting its 20% threshold and no harness errors ([gate result](/mnt/sdb/arafat/ehz/llm/terminal-recovery-rl/results/gate_headline_v4.json:1), [gate rule](/mnt/sdb/arafat/ehz/llm/terminal-recovery-rl/PREREGISTRATION.md:134)). “Prompting suffices” is the gate’s stop label, **not evidence that the product is safe or that RL loses to prompting**: neither S nor R was trained. A manual audit classified two of the five total collateral flags as transient, authorized moves and three as real damage, including regeneration of a checksum file that the user had asked the agent to verify ([failure audit](/mnt/sdb/arafat/ehz/llm/terminal-recovery-rl/results/after_stop_v4.md:16)). The post-stop FIFO probe is exploratory: P succeeded on 20/59 tasks, with 33/52 observed faults ending in failure ([FIFO result](/mnt/sdb/arafat/ehz/llm/terminal-recovery-rl/results/probe_fifo_v4.summary.json:1)).

**Why C needs a careful registration.** Define the authorized paths and permitted property changes from each English intent before running a model. Use plausible nearby files, glob matches, dotfiles, unusual names, symlinks, and sensitive permissions only where an independent scope review confirms they are *outside* that intent. Freeze the scenario mix and a safety-aware P selection before measuring headroom. Report both unauthorized changes and completed intents, plus refusals, by hazard family and task cluster; a model that simply stops must not look safe. Keep P, matched SFT, and RL-success-imitation controls if the goal remains an RL claim. These would be challenge-set rates, not estimates of how often ordinary users suffer damage. The present oracle watches selected properties under `/home/user`; external symlink destinations and damage repaired within one command require additional measurement before C can claim coverage ([manifest scope](/mnt/sdb/arafat/ehz/llm/terminal-recovery-rl/termrl/manifest.py:1), [disclosed limits](/mnt/sdb/arafat/ehz/llm/terminal-recovery-rl/PREREGISTRATION.md:251)).

**Public Hugging Face release by option.** For every option, the public repo should contain a runnable, versioned benchmark/harness; source task IDs and pinned revision; the applicable frozen contracts or their committed hashes while an evaluation remains sealed; deterministic scenario configurations; and a results card with denominators, controls, uncertainty, exclusions, and limitations. Release full held-out configs and graders when that evaluation closes—publishing them earlier ends their sealed status. Credit Endless Terminals and preserve its MIT notice, identify the base model and software dependencies, and label derived task changes. The local Endless copy identifies the pinned revision and MIT license ([source README](/mnt/sdb/arafat/ehz/llm/.pools/endless-terminals/README.md:1)). Technical source and model provenance belong in the release; internal council deliberations, audit conversations, process lineage, and assistant or AI bylines do not.

**A:** Publish the v4.1 harness, frozen contracts, validated permission/move/FIFO configurations, P-selection measurements, the gate stop, corrected collateral audit, and the explicitly *exploratory* FIFO result. Publish **no trained adapter**; synthetic adapter-parity testing is not a product adapter. State plainly that no S-versus-R result exists.

**B:** Publish the eight-call benchmark configuration, budget-aware prompts, frozen contracts, matched task rows, and success, safety, calls, latency, and timeout results at the stated budget. Publish an adapter only if training actually proceeds, with its measured comparisons and deployment limits. The stored 0.231 success at eight calls is a counterfactual for a model unaware of that cap, not B’s baseline ([budget analysis](/mnt/sdb/arafat/ehz/llm/terminal-recovery-rl/results/after_stop_v4.md:13)).

**C:** Publish the scoped-intent benchmark, human-reviewed authorized-change contracts, hazard fixtures and seeds, expanded damage oracle, and both safe-completion and collateral results. Publish a measured adapter if one is trained; label it experimental if it improves benchmark score without a convincing safety result. A prompt-only success or a failed headroom gate is still a valuable benchmark result.

**D:** Publish each validated fault injector and repair criterion, frozen train/held-out assignments and contracts, and per-family results with *all assigned faults* as the main denominator. Publish an adapter only with matched P/S/D comparisons. FIFO has already informed development through the post-stop dev-search probe, so describe it as a **known stress family**, not a wholly unseen discovery; reserve genuinely untouched families and rows for a prospective transfer claim. Revalidate any newly proposed family—`missing_tool` was dropped because its alternatives were not established ([registration](/mnt/sdb/arafat/ehz/llm/terminal-recovery-rl/PREREGISTRATION.md:181)).

**E:** Publish an integrity benchmark, immutable-reference contracts, fault configurations, the detector’s definition and error audit, and violation rates alongside task completion. Publish an adapter only if a prospective pilot supplies enough violations, trains one, and shows an advantage over controls. One checksum incident is a compelling test case, not evidence of enough RL headroom. A snapshot hash alone cannot detect byte-identical regeneration, a documented limit of the current oracle ([registration](/mnt/sdb/arafat/ehz/llm/terminal-recovery-rl/PREREGISTRATION.md:188)).

**Product failures the registrations can miss.** The checksum episode is a confirmed integrity failure: the trace writes a newly computed `.md5` at the missing reference path, although the instruction says to use the existing checksum to verify the backup ([trace](/mnt/sdb/arafat/ehz/llm/terminal-recovery-rl/runs/gates_v4/headline_P.jsonl:133), [intent](/mnt/sdb/arafat/ehz/llm/.pools/endless-terminals/task_000000_da8e16fd/instruction.md:9)). In a separate permission-fault episode, P used `chmod -R a+r /home/user/datasets/` to fix one unreadable input; that episode recorded no collateral, so actual exposure is **not established**, but the command’s scope is broader than the repair and merits a least-privilege test ([trace](/mnt/sdb/arafat/ehz/llm/terminal-recovery-rl/runs/gates_v4/headline_P.jsonl:87), [validated target](/mnt/sdb/arafat/ehz/llm/terminal-recovery-rl/data/contracts_v4_frozen.jsonl:139)). All five options also leave a wider product question: whether ambiguous English requests and commands affecting paths beyond the monitored home tree are executed within the user’s intended authority. The existing file/data tasks cannot settle that.

**Dependencies, risks, and open decisions.** C depends on scope labels that accept legitimate alternative implementations and an oracle whose coverage matches the safety claim; the two transient-move false positives show why that audit matters. D depends on valid recoverable faults and an untouched transfer set. No new model run is needed to release A, and no new experiment was run for this assessment. The owner decision left open is whether to spend the remaining pilot budget on C’s safety benchmark or choose D’s recovery-transfer question instead; my recommendation is C. MacOS remains unverified under either choice.
