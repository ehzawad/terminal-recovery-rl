## How to know a terminal-agent dataset is good (dataset-quality)

The defensible answer is: **we have evidence that the selected Endless Terminals tasks are executable and useful for controlled local-file experiments. We do not yet have evidence that this is a sufficiently diverse, realistic, challenging dataset for improving a general multi-step Linux agent.** Those are different claims. A dataset is good for RL when its tasks represent useful work, its rewards reliably distinguish correct and incorrect behavior, and the actual starting model still has learnable failures. Task count, a reference solution, and another model’s success rate do not establish those properties.

I treated the supplied project state as authoritative and verified the local manifests, validator, review records, and results. I performed read-only checks; I did not run new task containers, use the GPU, or alter X1. The thresholds below are proposed acceptance rules, except where explicitly identified as existing registered gates.

The verified ET evidence is stronger than “we generated examples,” but narrower than “the dataset is good.” Of the 609 easy-slice candidates, **593 passed operational validity: 97.4%**. That means no-op failure, repeated reference success, consistent recorded changes and test inventory, and safety-oracle checks. Reapplying the code-executing-test exclusion leaves **561 eligible tasks: 272 train, 78 dev_search, 64 dev_monitor, and 147 test**. There were 37 such exclusions among the original 609, including 32 otherwise-valid tasks; therefore 593 is a validated-contract count, not the final eligible-task count. These numbers reproduce from the manifests and match the [recorded slice description](/mnt/sdb/arafat/ehz/llm/terminal-recovery-rl/data/audit/council_r7/context.md:11) and [row-selection rules](/mnt/sdb/arafat/ehz/llm/terminal-recovery-rl/scripts/make_rows.py:77).

The hard-slice evidence shows why operational validity is insufficient. Its funnel was **171 candidates → 159 operationally valid → 139 passing validity and mutation discrimination → 84 admitted tasks in 83 groups**. Twenty of the 159 valid tasks failed discrimination, or **12.6%**. Review excluded **68/171, or 39.8%**: 34 had unstated tested requirements, 25 had instruction–test contradictions, 20 exposed answers, and five had other problems. Reasons overlap. Crucially, **55 review exclusions had passed both automated checks**. The final admission yield was **49.1%**, below that study’s required 144 groups. These are rates in a selected hard-candidate cohort, not prevalence estimates for all 2,492 ET tasks. Also, the eight reviewers covered separate batches: the files contain one review assignment per task, not eight independent judgments per task. See the [recorded outcome](/mnt/sdb/arafat/ehz/llm/terminal-recovery-rl/PREREGISTRATION.md:449) and [admission manifest](/mnt/sdb/arafat/ehz/llm/terminal-recovery-rl/data/hard/admission.json:1).

The target-model evidence is also material. The prompted base achieved **0.7756 macro safe success on 78 dev tasks**, with observed-fault recovery failure **6/40 = 15%**, below the registered 20% mechanism threshold. Thus aggregate headroom passed that study’s gate, but recovery-specific headroom did not. A budget-aware prompt subsequently achieved **28/32 = 87.5%** safe success in the eight-command precheck. The recent training probe comprised just **12 clean task groups, four trajectories each**; its three updates had **25%, 50%, and 25% informative groups**, totaling four informative groups out of twelve. That is limited evidence, but unfavorable evidence for efficient GRPO on this distribution. These results are different cohorts and conditions, not one interchangeable “78–88%” estimate. See the [registered stop](/mnt/sdb/arafat/ehz/llm/terminal-recovery-rl/PREREGISTRATION.md:255), [budget precheck](/mnt/sdb/arafat/ehz/llm/terminal-recovery-rl/results/kill_check_v5.summary.json:1), and [training probe](/mnt/sdb/arafat/ehz/llm/terminal-recovery-rl/results/grpo_update_probe.json:1).

Against that evidence, I would assess quality using the following measurable criteria.

**Task validity:** the pinned environment builds and resets reproducibly, starts in the intended unsolved state, has the necessary dependencies, and produces an interpretable grading result. Every admitted task should pass repeated reference runs and reject no-op; infrastructure failures must be reported separately from agent failures. **ET meets this operational criterion for the validated slice under its recorded harness.** It does not establish that every upstream task is valid or that another harness will preserve validity. The validator’s actual checks are documented in [validate_tasks.py](/mnt/sdb/arafat/ehz/llm/terminal-recovery-rl/scripts/validate_tasks.py:1).

**Grader discrimination:** measure both false acceptance of deliberately incorrect results and false rejection of legitimate alternative results. For each mandatory outcome, require at least one meaningful negative control, including plausible wrong content that preserves filenames, schema, and formatting. Every audited violation of a mandatory requirement must fail; every audited valid alternative must pass. Test deletion of outputs, altered values, missing records, wrong permissions where relevant, input-and-output co-modification, and reward-channel spoofing. **The hard slice passes only the limited mutation screen applied to its 84 admitted tasks; the broader candidate pool failed. The easy slice is unproven on comprehensive discrimination:** its merged validity records contain no discrimination results. Existing mutations cover selected output files and a few edits, not every possible semantic defect. A hidden test suite is not automatically a trustworthy reward function.

**Instruction–test consistency:** map every graded requirement to explicit instruction text or legitimately discoverable environment facts, and map every mandatory requested outcome to a check. Acceptance requires zero unresolved contradictions or secret requirements, and coverage of all mandatory outcomes. If a process requirement cannot be checked from final state, either observe the process or disclose that it is unverified. **The hard candidates clearly failed this criterion; the retained 84 passed their recorded review. The easy slice has not received an equivalent complete review.** One excluded task requests a Unicode arrow while tests require ASCII, and tests impose backup modes absent from the instruction. That is a specification defect, not evidence of inadequate Linux skill. See the [concrete review evidence](/mnt/sdb/arafat/ehz/llm/terminal-recovery-rl/data/hard/review/batch_0.json:106).

**Answer exposure:** distinguish necessary task inputs and harmless output-schema examples from a complete answer or solver made available to the agent. Inspect the built filesystem, environment variables, readable setup files, copied metadata, references, tests, expected-output files, and relevant history. The acceptance threshold is zero confirmed answer/solver exposure in admitted tasks. Counterfactual fixtures should change the correct output so that copying an example cannot remain sufficient. **ET has screened exposure, not certified absence:** the earlier static filter removed 170 literal-block candidates, and hard-slice review still found 20 exposed-answer tasks. Static flags need adjudication; complete input shown in a prompt can weaken environment dependence without being a secret-answer leak. See the [filtering and exposure findings](/mnt/sdb/arafat/ehz/llm/terminal-recovery-rl/data/audit/AUDIT_REPORT.md:21).

**Solvability:** require a demonstrated solution using the agent’s permitted tools, privileges, dependencies, and actual time/action limits. For every admitted fault configuration, demonstrate that the fault causes a genuine failure and that a permitted repair restores success without collateral damage. **ET has good evidence of reference feasibility and validated repair configurations, but only conditional evidence of policy-budget solvability.** The validator runs reference scripts with a 180-second command allowance, whereas policy commands normally receive 30 seconds. Reference success therefore does not by itself prove that every task fits the deployed interaction budget. See the [reference runner](/mnt/sdb/arafat/ehz/llm/terminal-recovery-rl/scripts/validate_tasks.py:79) and [policy limits](/mnt/sdb/arafat/ehz/llm/terminal-recovery-rl/termrl/config.py:39).

**Difficulty spread and target-model headroom:** measure safe success, substantive failure types, and within-task reward variation for the exact starting checkpoint, strong prompt, sampling distribution, and deployment budget. Report easy, intermediate, and unsolved groups separately; an overall 50% success rate can hide a mixture of always-solved and never-solved tasks that supplies little group-relative signal. For this project, retain the existing gates: macro safe success **20–80%**, task-cluster 95% upper bound **below 90%**, semantic-reward variation with Wilson 95% lower bound **above 60%**, and **at least 20%** of groups containing both complete successes and failures. A recovery claim additionally needs **at least 20% substantive recovery failures** among the defined eligible opportunities. **Current ET fails to demonstrate adequate recovery-specific and GRPO headroom.** The 84 admitted hard tasks have not been calibrated on Qwen; their o3 rates cannot substitute. These thresholds are local investment rules for this training method, not universal definitions of dataset quality. See the [registered gates](/mnt/sdb/arafat/ehz/llm/terminal-recovery-rl/PREREGISTRATION.md:74).

**Diversity and deduplication:** count independent source/problem families, not UUIDs, paraphrases, fault variants, or repeated trajectories. Cluster using source identity, normalized instructions, fixtures, solution structure, and grader structure, then adjudicate suspicious pairs. Require zero known family overlap across train and claimed held-out evaluation. Report domain proportions and effective root counts. **ET does not establish independence or broad diversity.** Its original lexical detector missed a known related pair with cosine similarity only 0.1762; later review found six hard–hard and 30 hard–easy variant pairs. A random task-ID split therefore cannot be described as a verified problem-family split. See the [deduplication limitation](/mnt/sdb/arafat/ehz/llm/terminal-recovery-rl/data/audit/AUDIT_REPORT.md:25) and [later variant findings](/mnt/sdb/arafat/ehz/llm/terminal-recovery-rl/PREREGISTRATION.md:456).

**Realism relative to user intents:** annotate whether each task represents a plausible user outcome, whether environmental observations matter, whether failures are natural or injected, and which deployment intent family it serves. Compare those proportions with a separately collected, anonymized intent sample. A useful provisional threshold is **at least 80% of reviewed tasks mapping to plausible intended user work**, with **at least half of the multi-step training allocation requiring state-dependent investigation, action, or recovery**. Do not enforce a minimum command count: a competent agent may batch work. **ET is plausible for a narrow local file/data stratum, but representativeness of general Linux work is unestablished.** Its local/standard-library filtering and injected faults deliberately narrow the distribution. A broad final mixture should cover several intended families—files, data/logs, permissions, processes/services, packages/environments, and configuration/debugging—with weights determined by intended use. Each contributing source need not cover every family.

**Safety:** measure unauthorized modifications, input fabrication, permission changes, and grader interference, while testing that legitimate solutions are not wrongly penalized. Every audited destructive control must be caught and every audited authorized alternative accepted. Treat task-image construction and grading as untrusted-code boundaries; container execution alone is insufficient. **ET provisionally meets a bounded local-filesystem safety criterion**, supported by planted fixtures, property-level checks, isolated grading, and code-executing-test exclusions. It does not certify general Linux safety. The oracle excludes several properties and paths outside `/home/user`, and does not observe damage restored within one call. Two of four initial clean collateral flags were false positives, illustrating why safety-oracle specificity matters as well as sensitivity. See the [limitations](/mnt/sdb/arafat/ehz/llm/terminal-recovery-rl/PREREGISTRATION.md:251) and [false-positive audit](/mnt/sdb/arafat/ehz/llm/terminal-recovery-rl/results/after_stop_v4.md:17). Excluding executable-artifact graders is a limitation of this harness, not a general requirement that good terminal datasets avoid programming tasks.

**Licence and provenance:** require a pinned source revision, recorded licence, permitted intended use, retained notices, and provenance for redistributed fixtures and third-party material. Acceptance means **100% of included records have resolved provenance and licensing**, with unresolved records quarantined. **ET meets the declared dataset-licence criterion:** its publisher identifies it as MIT. That declaration is not an exhaustive third-party-asset audit. [Publisher’s dataset card](https://huggingface.co/datasets/obiwan96/endless-terminals).

**Contamination:** distinguish exposed answers during execution, train/test family overlap, benchmark-derived training content, and possible base-model pretraining exposure. Require zero known training overlap with evaluation roots, fixtures, and solutions, after documented exact and semantic screening. **ET’s contamination status is unresolved:** task-ID separation exists, but verified family independence and absence from Qwen’s pretraining are not established. Public availability is not itself disqualifying for training. It limits what can be claimed from evaluation, especially when success can reflect remembered answers. Use fresh held-out problem families and changed fixtures; renaming files alone is insufficient.

The cheapest credible audit is a staged rejection funnel. First, perform **whole-corpus static screening before any model rollout**: inventory required files, pin revisions and image identities, extract licences and source roots, flag answer exposure, inspect grader entry points, identify unsupported dependencies and privileges, and deduplicate against both the candidate corpus and intended evaluation sets. Freeze an exclusion ledger with reasons and denominators. Zero unresolved licence, exposed-answer, known evaluation-overlap, or grader-integrity defects are allowed into the admitted subset. Report usable yield separately from retained-task quality; a source with many rejected records may still supply a useful narrow subset.

Second, perform a **CPU-only executable and specification audit**, starting with a small diverse canary batch and stopping promptly on systematic defects. Once the plumbing works, deeply audit **60 randomly selected independent roots after static filtering**, plus separately reported suspicious cases. Run no-op, two fresh reference executions, semantic wrong-output controls, safety controls, and instruction–test mapping. Require zero unresolved critical defects in that random sample before bulk admission. Under simple independent sampling and reliable defect detection, zero defects in 60 gives a one-sided 95% upper bound of about **4.9%** on residual defect prevalence. It does not prove perfection or compensate for an ineffective reviewer. If defects reveal a generator-wide pattern, fix the filter/conversion and audit a fresh sample. ET’s hard-slice findings justify per-task specification review for that slice rather than assuming a small clean sample certifies it.

Third, **run automated admission checks on every task actually selected for training**, reusing cached builds and restoring snapshots between controls. Every included task must pass reference/no-op, applicable semantic mutations, exposure screening, and safety checks; unresolved failures are excluded. Apply detailed review to every flagged task and all members of affected defect families. Use the actual native reward: a successful wrapper or pytest process need not mean task success. This is a demonstrated issue in the local transfer audit, where 40 TBLite graders could pass pytest regardless of their recorded task score. [Native-reward finding](/mnt/sdb/arafat/ehz/llm/terminal-recovery-rl/results/transfer_readiness.md:13). Sampling is an economical way to decide whether to integrate a source; it is not a reason to skip inexpensive checks on admitted records.

Fourth, only after those checks, run **48 independent audited roots × four attempts** with the actual starting model and strong prompt, at intended RL sampling settings. Freeze the sample and gates before observing outcomes. For 48 groups, **36 informative groups** are needed for the two-sided Wilson 95% lower endpoint to exceed 60%; require at least **10 mixed-success groups** to meet the 20% rule. Apply the headroom and substantive-failure gates above, and report infrastructure errors separately, targeting **below 1%** with no unresolved systematic failure. At the measured 34.5 seconds per trajectory, 192 trajectories are approximately **1.84 hours**; this is an ET-based planning estimate, not a promise for service-heavy tasks. Expand borderline evidence once using a predeclared larger sample. Clear failure should send the dataset to revision, a different curriculum, or verified SFT—not automatically into a full RL run.

This protocol establishes “reasonable to try training,” not “training will improve the product.” Demonstrating that requires subsequent paired evaluation against the prompted base on untouched, independently grouped tasks, with safe success and uncertainty reported. Demonstrating an RL-specific advantage needs appropriate SFT controls. X1 remains an owner-authorized exploratory experiment; its completion or publication cannot retroactively certify the dataset or erase the earlier gates.

Stack Exchange-derived tasks need additional screening because **a real question provides evidence of a real problem, but an accepted answer does not provide an executable environment or a trustworthy reward**. Preserve the underlying user goal while reconstructing shell, distribution, tool versions, permissions, filesystem state, and relevant service state. Reject or separately route opinion questions, missing-state questions, hardware/kernel-dependent situations, and tasks requiring unavailable live services. A straightforward one-command question may be valuable for SFT or a command curriculum; adding artificial steps does not make it a good multi-step RL task.

Accepted answers, votes, and tags should be ranking signals only. Reproduce candidate answers, inspect comments and revisions for corrections, and check common portability and safety failures: GNU/BSD differences, quoting, whitespace and unusual filenames, locale dependence, recursive deletion, privilege assumptions, and obsolete options. Derive the grader from the reconstructed user requirement, independently of the accepted answer’s exact implementation. Otherwise the conversion can encode the same mistake into prompt, solution, and checker. Include another valid implementation where practical and plausible incorrect implementations that the grader must reject.

Keep accepted answers and conversion scaffolding out of the agent-visible environment. Preserve useful examples, but change fixtures so those examples are insufficient to solve the task by copying. Keep all answers, revisions, duplicate-question links, cross-posts, and evolved variants of one underlying problem in a single family. The existing SETA inventory illustrates the distinction: approximately **4,567 records correspond to 3,465 source-root groups**, before establishing deeper semantic independence. [Local inventory]( /mnt/sdb/arafat/ehz/llm/terminal-recovery-rl/data/audit/AUDIT_REPORT.md:13). Screen those families against evaluation sources before partitioning.

The owner’s acceptance of CC BY-SA 4.0 resolves that preference; it does not mean every historical post is version 4.0. Stack Overflow specifies **2.5 before 2011-04-08, 3.0 from then until 2018-05-02, and 4.0 thereafter**, with the applicable licence recorded per revision. Preserve source URLs, post and revision IDs, attribution, licence information, and transformation notices; distribute derived content in accordance with its applicable share-alike terms. The cheapest initial licensing scope is verified 4.0 revisions, with earlier versions handled separately. [Stack Overflow licensing](https://stackoverflow.com/help/licensing), [CC BY-SA 4.0 terms](https://creativecommons.org/licenses/by-sa/4.0/). Remove secrets and unnecessary personal information from reconstructed fixtures. Dataset redistribution and model-weight licensing should be tracked as separate questions.

The dependencies are a reproducible admission harness, source-family manifests, a defined target-intent distribution, and an evaluation set with credible independence. The main remaining risks are correlated task families, graders and references sharing mistakes, reviewers missing defects, unverified pretraining exposure, and model-dependent headroom changing after prompting or SFT. Open owner decisions are the intended Linux-use distribution, acceptable residual audit uncertainty, treatment of older Stack Exchange licence versions, and whether a source that supplies useful SFT but fails GRPO gates should still be retained. None requires interrupting the already-authorized experimental X1 run.
