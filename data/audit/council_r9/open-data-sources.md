## Open data for a multi-step Linux terminal agent: what exists and what fits (open-data-sources)

There is material open data beyond Endless Terminals. **The strongest next candidates are audited subsets of SETA, TMax-15K and LiteCoder-Terminal-RL.** Stack Overflow and Unix & Linux Stack Exchange can seed executable RL tasks, but accepted answers alone provide neither an environment nor a trustworthy reward.

I saved the complete survey, including per-dataset licences, dates, revisions, byte sizes, contamination findings and offline requirements, in [REPORT.md](/mnt/sdb/arafat/ehz/llm/terminal-recovery-rl/data/audit/council_r9/open-data-survey/REPORT.md). The accompanying evidence includes downloaded primary sources, inventories, sampled task files and overlap results. Checks covered 81 source hashes and all local report links. **This was a source and static audit; I did not certify new environments by executing them or change the training run.** Findings are current to September 28, 2026.

**Endless Terminals:** the pinned January 24 release contains **2,492 Harbor tasks**, about **406 MB**, declared **MIT**. The paper’s 3,255 count refers to Apptainer tasks, approximately 2,500 of which were converted to Harbor. It supplies direct RL rewards through executable environments and tests. It overlaps the project’s existing data directly; additional variants cannot constitute independent evaluation. Its authors say generation preceded TB2.0, but that does not independently establish semantic separation. The selected local subset already has offline adaptations. [Dataset](https://huggingface.co/datasets/obiwan96/endless-terminals), [paper](https://arxiv.org/html/2601.16443v1).

The project’s own evidence explains why further filtering matters: the hard-tail audit admitted **84 tasks/83 groups**, with **20 graders failing mutation discrimination**. Of the 68 tasks rejected by instruction–test review, **55 had passed both automated checks**. Reference success and no-op failure therefore do not establish a good reward. Combined with the prompted base’s strong performance, this supports seeking valid new task families rather than treating low published success rates as evidence of useful difficulty. [Admission record](/mnt/sdb/arafat/ehz/llm/terminal-recovery-rl/PREREGISTRATION.md:449).

**SETA:** the July 1 snapshot contains **4,567 environments**, **2.11 GB**, declared **Apache-2.0**: 3,255 synthesized tasks and 1,312 evolved variants. Its sources include **1,140 Stack Overflow, 562 Unix.SE and 1,796 Ask Ubuntu records**, counting variants. Operational grouping yields approximately **3,465 roots**, not 4,567 independent problems. It supplies Docker environments, reference solutions and checkers, so it supports direct RL. Preserve upstream Q&A attribution and applicable CC BY-SA terms; the Apache declaration does not erase those origins. Offline work includes caching packages, replacing downloading verifier wrappers and adapting local service startup. The previous service sample found fetching/installing commands in **33 of 34 verifiers**. Group question descendants together, including its NL2Bash-derived tasks; no comprehensive ET/TB semantic clearance was established. [Card](https://huggingface.co/datasets/camel-ai/SETA-Env), [pipeline](https://github.com/camel-ai/seta).

**TMax-15K:** the June 23 snapshot contains **14,601 tasks**, **513 MB**, under **ODC-BY**, with additional generator-output notices in its card. It supports direct RL, but canonical Apptainer definitions, fixtures and tests need Docker adaptation. Author-local fixture paths require reconstruction from the archive. The earlier audit identified **213 candidate repair tasks**, not admitted tasks, and found exposed expected answers in six of seven deliberately suspicious samples. That is a demonstrated failure mode, not a prevalence estimate. [Card](https://huggingface.co/datasets/allenai/TMax-15K), [retained leaking construction](/mnt/sdb/arafat/ehz/llm/terminal-recovery-rl/data/audit/council_r9/open-data-survey/sources/tmax_answer_leak_example.def:64).

TMax provides particularly relevant published evidence: Qwen3.5-9B improved from **16.1 to 28.8 on TB2.1**, versus 25.5 with ET RL. Its tested SFT mixtures did not consistently improve that base. However, the training used substantially greater compute and longer horizons than this LoRA experiment. Its published 13-token audit found zero overlap with TB2.0/TBLite; this does not establish semantic independence or offline feasibility. [Paper, Tables 2, 7 and 11](https://arxiv.org/html/2606.23321v1).

**LiteCoder-Terminal:** distinguish the **602 RL environments, 155 MB**, from **11,255 SFT trajectories, 1.20 GB**. Both releases declare **MIT**; checked revisions are June 26 and June 1 respectively. Only the environment release supplies direct rewards. All 602 environments declare internet access and zero GPUs; 601 request at most 8 GiB RAM. All eight systematically sampled verifier wrappers download dependencies. Some local Git/service tasks appear adaptable, but this remains untested. I found **zero exact normalized instruction matches** against local ET, TB2.1 and TBLite; paraphrase and shared-solution contamination remain unchecked. Its longer trajectories also challenge the current 16-command budget. [RL card](https://huggingface.co/datasets/Lite-Coder/LiteCoder-Terminal-RL-preview), [SFT card](https://huggingface.co/datasets/Lite-Coder/LiteCoder-Terminal-SFT), [measured evidence](/mnt/sdb/arafat/ehz/llm/terminal-recovery-rl/data/audit/council_r9/open-data-survey/measured_inventory.json).

**Other executable candidates:** CLI-Gym’s February release has **1,655 instances across 29 repositories**, under **Apache-2.0** for data and MIT for generator code. It damages working environments and rewards recovery through tests—a useful match for this project. Its roughly 4 MB metadata excludes substantial base images and fixtures; verify their availability and protect graders from runtime modifications. OpenThoughts-Agent-v1-RL has **728 packed NL2Bash-derived tasks**, while TermiGen’s public repository contains **3,566 task configurations**. Both are technically executable, but I could not establish a dataset-wide licence for either checked release. Hold them pending clarification. Their source families and generated variants also require grouping. [CLI-Gym](https://huggingface.co/datasets/LiberCoders/CLI-Gym), [OpenThoughts](https://huggingface.co/datasets/open-thoughts/OpenThoughts-Agent-v1-RL), [TermiGen](https://github.com/ucsb-mlsec/terminal-bench-env).

**Terminal-World requires a naming distinction.** Cheng et al.’s May 20 paper reports **5,723 environments**, but I could not verify a public downloadable task corpus and dataset licence. SkillSynth’s reported **3,560 tasks** and September’s Environment Evolution likewise remain methods rather than verified acquisition inventory in this survey. Separately, **EuniAI/TerminalWorld** releases **1,530 benchmark tasks**, including 200 human-verified tasks, about **929 MB**. Its current licence is **CC BY 4.0**; the card explicitly identifies older NC row labels as stale. Preserve it for potential evaluation, subject to offline and lineage admission. [Terminal-World paper](https://arxiv.org/html/2605.20876v1), [SkillSynth](https://arxiv.org/html/2604.25727v1), [Environment Evolution](https://arxiv.org/html/2609.04128v1), [separate TerminalWorld benchmark](https://huggingface.co/datasets/EuniAI/TerminalWorld).

**Additional trajectory data:** Nemotron-Terminal-Corpus releases **366,154 SFT trajectories**, about **8.20 GB**, under **CC BY 4.0**. The checked release does not provide RL sandboxes. TerminalTraj’s public release has **20,000 text trajectories**, about **164 MB**, despite larger paper counts; I did not verify its dataset licence or runnable environments in that release. Neither should be counted as ready direct-RL inventory. Their source adaptations and benchmark overlap require inspection before SFT. [Nemotron](https://huggingface.co/datasets/nvidia/Nemotron-Terminal-Corpus), [TerminalTraj](https://huggingface.co/datasets/m-a-p/TerminalTraj).

**Command knowledge has a different role.** NL2Bash’s standard 2018 split contains **9,305 English–command pairs**; its data has a specific **MIT** licence, separate from GPL-licensed code. It supports targeted SFT, not execution rewards without fixtures and checkers. NLC2CMD, the 2020 competition, largely reformats that source rather than adding an independent multi-step corpus. Its documentation describes over 10,000 pairs and over 36,000 parsed manual records; evaluation eventually contained 1,867 examples. Licensing for added examples and manuals needs separate checking. Their shared ancestry creates overlap with SETA, InterCode and OpenThoughts. [NL2Bash](https://github.com/TellinaTool/nl2bash), [data licence](https://github.com/TellinaTool/nl2bash/blob/master/data/bash/LICENSE), [NLC2CMD documentation](https://github.com/IBM/clai/blob/nlc2cmd/docs/nl2bash-data.md).

**InterCode-Bash is executable**, contrary to its grouping in the brief. The MIT-licensed 2023 release contains **200 tasks** with concrete filesystems and gold execution. However, source inspection found a reward combining Git changes, file hashes and TF-IDF output similarity; its content-check filter omits modified-file status `M`. These are static weaknesses, not a newly executed exploit. Harden its semantic checks and episode resets before optimizing against it. Its NL2Bash ancestry prevents independent evaluation against that same source family. [Repository](https://github.com/princeton-nlp/intercode), [pinned reward implementation](https://github.com/princeton-nlp/intercode/blob/c3e46d827cfc9d4c704ec078f7abf9f41e3191d8/intercode/envs/bash/bash_env.py#L89).

**tldr-pages and manuals are most immediately useful as offline references.** The checked tldr tree has **7,550 English pages**, including **6,762 common/Linux pages**, under **CC BY 4.0**; English content totals about 4.36 MB. Examples can become SFT pairs but lack environments and checkers. Linux man-pages **6.19, August 25, 2026**, contains 3,132 page entries, including 1,590 aliases, in a 1.91 MB archive. It covers kernel/libc interfaces, not all Linux commands, and uses per-file licences. Neither supplies direct RL rewards; match documentation to sandbox package versions. Generic shared command knowledge is expected, while copied benchmark tasks require exclusion. [tldr](https://github.com/tldr-pages/tldr), [official man-pages releases](https://www.kernel.org/pub/linux/docs/man-pages/).

**Stack Exchange can become RL data through environment construction.** The checked public April 2024 archive is approximately **706 MB compressed for Unix.SE**; Stack Overflow’s Posts archive alone is **23.03 GB**. Raw Q&A supplies SFT material or task seeds. Licences are revision-dependent **CC BY-SA 2.5, 3.0 or 4.0**, not uniformly 4.0. Preserve authors, source/revision links, licence and modification notices. There is also an acquisition constraint: today’s official account-based dump download requires affirming no intended LLM training. Do not make that affirmation for this project; use an appropriately obtained public snapshot with suitable access terms. [Archive metadata](https://archive.org/metadata/stackexchange), [licensing policy](https://stackoverflow.com/help/licensing), [current download procedure](https://stackoverflow.com/help/data-dumps).

The conversion procedure is concrete: select a reproducible question; explicitly resolve missing assumptions; construct a pinned broken Docker environment; provide local fixtures and package caches; independently replay the accepted answer and valid alternatives; then grade stated outcomes with protected semantic checks. For an Nginx problem, check configuration validity, fresh loopback requests, logs, restart behavior and preservation of unrelated sites. Do not grade command-string agreement. No-op and wrong mutations must fail; fresh reference runs must pass. Accepted answers can be outdated, incomplete or incorrect, while generated tests can share the same mistaken assumptions as generated solutions.

This has established precedents. **SETA directly converts Stack Overflow, Unix.SE and Ask Ubuntu content into environments and tests**; its separate rollout review found 94 design flaws that earlier checks missed. **InterCode and OpenThoughts** demonstrate narrower NL2Bash-to-executable conversions. These support the feasibility of conversion, not blanket validity of resulting rewards. [SETA methods](https://arxiv.org/html/2607.10891v1), [InterCode](https://arxiv.org/abs/2306.14898), [OpenThoughts release](https://huggingface.co/datasets/open-thoughts/OpenThoughts-Agent-v1-RL).

For this box, the remaining dependency is an admission pass: offline builds and resets, reference replay, instruction–checker review, leakage inspection, mutation discrimination, resource measurements and prompted-base rollouts. The native Terminal-Bench grading path executes tests inside the task container; arbitrary imported RL tasks need stronger protection against mutable tools and reward files. Split source questions and evolution families before sampling. Exact text deduplication alone is insufficient. Keep TB/TBLite out of training; the local feasibility audit already shows substantial reference and offline limitations. [Native grader](/mnt/sdb/arafat/ehz/llm/terminal-recovery-rl/termrl/tb.py:205), [transfer audit](/mnt/sdb/arafat/ehz/llm/terminal-recovery-rl/results/transfer_readiness.md:1).

Open decisions are the curation/build budget, whether horizons may exceed 16 commands, and whether service/runtime repair merits the additional grading isolation. A practical first queue is **24 SETA, 16 TMax and eight LiteCoder tasks**. These are proposed audit allocations, not established valid counts. Measure genuine mixed-success rollout groups and independent held-out improvement before claiming training value.

My ranked recommendation is **1. SETA’s local Linux troubleshooting subset; 2. TMax repair/debugging/build tasks; 3. LiteCoder’s local CPU-only RL tasks, with selective verified SFT if needed; 4. CLI-Gym for runtime recovery; 5. newly constructed Unix.SE tasks, followed by Linux-focused Stack Overflow, to fill measured gaps; 6. NL2Bash/NLC2CMD, tldr and matching manuals for targeted SFT or reference access.** Retain ET for regression and X1 continuity, preserve external benchmarks for evaluation, and hold unresolved-licence or unverified-release datasets outside the training inventory.
