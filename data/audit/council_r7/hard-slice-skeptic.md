## Skeptic: selection effects, broken verifiers and the stop-or-go call (hard-slice-skeptic)

There are material problems in this lens. My recommendation is **stop and ship the two stopped studies and the harness now**. H1 could support a separately registered, narrowly scoped pilot, but it is not ready for another training attempt. H2 is a plausible transfer evaluation, not evidence that moving to another scoreboard will rescue the intended claim. Two stops do not make further research dishonest; continuing until some task distribution produces “RL demonstrably fixed it,” while suppressing the unsuccessful searches, would.

The existing registration supports shipping: Study 2’s kill rule explicitly says to stop and ship at ≥27/32, and the recorded result is 28/32. Study 1 also stopped under its rule, including after the collateral-oracle correction. Neither establishes that RL is ineffective: no substantive S/R comparison was conducted. The defensible result is that the proposed comparisons failed their development-stage prerequisites. See [Study 1 outcome](/mnt/sdb/arafat/ehz/llm/terminal-recovery-rl/PREREGISTRATION.md:255), [Study 2 stopping rule](/mnt/sdb/arafat/ehz/llm/terminal-recovery-rl/PREREGISTRATION.md:300), and [Study 2 outcome](/mnt/sdb/arafat/ehz/llm/terminal-recovery-rl/PREREGISTRATION.md:344).

My review was read-only: source inspection, local metadata calculations, archived trajectory inspection, and primary-source browsing. I ran no Qwen evaluations, training, containers, or task solutions and changed no files.

The strongest new evidence is an actual hard-tail task whose teacher difficulty partly measures a specification defect. Task `dd9a29de` has o3 success **10/16**. Its instruction explicitly requires an alert template with a space between `%` and the comma, while its example and exact-match grader require no space. Three archived o3 failures—zero-based runs 1, 8, and 9—failed only that comparison, with six other tests passing. Accepting the expressly stated spacing would change the archived count to **13/16**, outside H1’s proposed cutoff. That is a counterfactual interpretation of existing grading records, not a rerun. The conflicting [instruction](/mnt/sdb/arafat/ehz/llm/.pools/endless-terminals/task_000000_dd9a29de/instruction.md:45), [grader expectation](/mnt/sdb/arafat/ehz/llm/.pools/endless-terminals/task_000000_dd9a29de/tests/test_final_state.py:35), and [first affected failure](/mnt/sdb/arafat/ehz/llm/.pools/endless-terminals/task_000000_dd9a29de/solution/o3_summary.json:81) establish the problem directly. This task passes the broad local screen; I have not established its admission under every later H1 filter.

The same example exposes two further weaknesses. A local comparison found that its reference script body is identical to the commands from the first successful archived o3 rollout. Reference success therefore supplies no independent resolution of the ambiguity. Also, its two “sorted” tests inspect the grader’s **expected constants**, not the submitted files. Those tests cannot detect an unsorted submission themselves. This does not defeat the separate exact-content checks, but it contaminates partial credit: five of seven checks can pass for existing, world-readable output files whose contents are entirely wrong. See the [reference](/mnt/sdb/arafat/ehz/llm/.pools/endless-terminals/task_000000_dd9a29de/solution/solve.sh:5), [constant-only tests](/mnt/sdb/arafat/ehz/llm/.pools/endless-terminals/task_000000_dd9a29de/tests/test_final_state.py:109), and [reward calculation](/mnt/sdb/arafat/ehz/llm/terminal-recovery-rl/termrl/env.py:277). A nonzero or varying reward need not represent progress on the requested transformation.

I independently reproduced **611** tasks with o3 ≤11/16 and **489** passing the original broad local screen. I did not verify the approximately 190-task final-candidate estimate. More importantly, the hard tail is not wholly unseen: archived Qwen logs contain **20 hard-tail task IDs across 80 episodes**, and the original probe manifest reserves **25 hard-tail IDs as development-only**, including tasks without completed Qwen traces. Preserve all those reservations, not just the nine probe overlaps in the old easy slice. The manifest explicitly says “DEV-only forever” at [probe_tasks.txt:1](/mnt/sdb/arafat/ehz/llm/terminal-recovery-rl/data/probe_tasks.txt:1). A newly computed hash cannot erase prior exposure.

The methodological inference is that low o3 success selects a mixture of genuine difficulty, brittle conventions, ambiguous requirements, environment problems, and verifier errors. Sixteen attempts also provide a noisy difficulty estimate. The tail may be enriched for defects, but this review does not estimate that enrichment or establish that most hard tasks are invalid. Conversely, one or more teacher successes do not certify validity: a successful trajectory may merely have guessed the grader’s preferred interpretation. The previous static audit already documented substantive verifier weaknesses in the easier population and warned that surviving its filters did not establish soundness. See [AUDIT_REPORT.md:29](/mnt/sdb/arafat/ehz/llm/terminal-recovery-rl/data/audit/AUDIT_REPORT.md:29).

**Legitimate H1 selection** would freeze the pinned source, o3 ≤11/16 rule, non-difficulty filters, semantic-admission rubric, exclusions, and partition procedure before any *new* Qwen measurement. Published o3 difficulty is a legitimate sampling variable independent of this experiment’s Qwen outcomes. Blinded exclusions for demonstrable ambiguity, wrong grading, infeasible dependencies, or unsuitable scope are also legitimate. The resulting claim must concern that explicitly selected population. It cannot silently become a claim about all Endless Terminals tasks or ordinary terminal use. Training-only difficulty filtering or curricula are permissible after partitioning, with the sampling policy and control opportunities disclosed.

**Illegitimate primary-test selection** includes choosing tasks because Qwen failed, retaining P failures while discarding P successes, retaining R wins, changing the o3 threshold after seeing Qwen headroom, trying hash salts until a promising split appears, or dropping “broken” tasks preferentially when they hurt R. Selecting from the twelve known always-failed development tasks would produce a diagnostic challenge set, not an untouched test. An explicitly registered baseline-failure-conditioned study can be meaningful, but it answers a conditional question and needs independent qualification and evaluation attempts; it cannot reuse the qualifying failures as the comparison baseline or replace H1’s prospective primary test.

Hash partitioning is legitimate only after respecting existing exposure and semantic relationships. Group near-duplicates, shared solution skeletons, and parameterized variants before hashing; variants do not become independent roots because their UUIDs differ. Check relationships across the hard slice and easy retention set as well. The earlier audit found that its text-similarity detector missed an obvious related pair, so singleton hash groups are not proof of independence. See [AUDIT_REPORT.md:25](/mnt/sdb/arafat/ehz/llm/terminal-recovery-rl/data/audit/AUDIT_REPORT.md:25). Freeze one salt and assignment, reserve distinct prompt-search, gate, checkpoint-selection, and test roles, and report attrition. Repeated episodes improve measurement of a task; they do not replenish an inadequate number of independent tasks.

The hard tail needs substantially more validity work than the existing gate. That gate checks no-op failure, repeated reference success, matching reference write sets and test inventories, planted-fixture damage detection, and fault behavior. These are useful operational checks. They do not establish agreement between the instruction and verifier, acceptance of alternative correct solutions, or rejection of meaningful wrong answers. See [validate_tasks.py:90](/mnt/sdb/arafat/ehz/llm/terminal-recovery-rl/scripts/validate_tasks.py:90).

First, audit each admitted evaluation task against a written requirement-to-check mapping. Determine what a competent solver can infer from the instruction and visible environment without consulting the hidden tests. Resolve contradictory templates, unstated rounding or ordering rules, missing inputs, and restrictions that the evaluator cannot observe. For tasks admitting multiple implementations, test an independently constructed valid alternative. Exact formatting is legitimate when clearly specified and consequential to the requested interface; learning a hidden choice between conflicting formats is not evidence of the intended competence. Material repairs create a versioned derived task. Its old o3 score remains historical metadata, not a calibrated difficulty measurement for the repaired version.

Second, test verifier discrimination with deliberately wrong artifacts: missing and duplicated records, wrong joins or aggregates, swapped fields, plausible constant outputs, corrupted archives, fabricated checksums, and modified source inputs where relevant. Tests must derive expectations from protected original data, not agent-modifiable purported truth. Include harmless alternative representations where the specification permits them. Audit partial credit separately: constant-only, existence-only, and redundant checks can create apparent “informative groups” without semantic progress. The existing reward-variance gate cannot substitute for that audit.

Third, independently review the safety contract. It currently derives permitted property changes from reference executions. That can reject a legitimate alternative workflow or permit an incidental reference-side modification that the user never authorized. Validate permitted actions against the task specification, then exercise legitimate intermediate transitions and unauthorized changes. The previous directory-to-symlink false positives demonstrate why reference success plus one fixture-deletion self-test is insufficient. The relevant contract derivation is at [validate_tasks.py:95](/mnt/sdb/arafat/ehz/llm/terminal-recovery-rl/scripts/validate_tasks.py:95), with enforcement at [manifest.py:68](/mnt/sdb/arafat/ehz/llm/terminal-recovery-rl/termrl/manifest.py:68).

Fourth, add evaluation evidence that separates conventions from transferable competence. Where applicable, freeze input perturbations—renamed identifiers, reordered records, changed values, boundary cases—with independently recomputed answers before training. Keep some checks unavailable to reward optimization. At final evaluation, blind the adjudicator to arm identity and examine both directions of disagreement, plus samples of shared successes and failures. Auditing only R wins misses false failures that depress P and shared exploits that inflate every arm. A defect discovered after evaluation must trigger a symmetric, documented adjudication or rescoring policy. Do not quietly delete unfavorable tasks and promote on the survivors.

H1 also needs a **new question and mechanism definition**. Clean hard-task completion is different from recovery after injected faults or finishing within eight commands. “Rerun the ladder” cannot mean carrying forward recovery incidence or the budget mechanism without their triggering conditions. Register a meaningful clean-task competence endpoint and, if a mechanism claim is desired, an observable error category before measurement. Keep competitive prompting, S-before-R, D, retention, and the promotion requirements. If the repaired tasks become easy, or S removes the remaining headroom, stop. That is successful identification of why training was unnecessary, not a reason to make the tasks harsher.

For H2, the literature supplies motivation but not the required result. TMax reports Endless-trained Qwen3.5-9B gains of **10.7 points on TBLite and 9.4 on TB2.1 over the unadapted model**. Those are below the project’s twelve-point threshold even against that baseline, and they do not compare against this project’s P/S/D controls. The experiment used much larger training resources and budgets; checkpoints were selected using TBLite. It therefore does not establish this LoRA study’s feasibility, attribution, or likely promotion. [TMax, experimental setup and Table 2](https://arxiv.org/html/2606.23321v1#S4).

External evaluation can reduce dependence on the ET grader family, but it does not confer validity automatically. Terminal-Bench’s own 2.1 announcement documents fixes to **28 of 89** tasks, including dependency drift, insufficient resource budgets, and instruction/test disagreement; one task requested PostgreSQL while testing Spark SQL. [Terminal-Bench 2.1 release notes](https://www.tbench.ai/news/terminal-bench-2-1). Pin a specific benchmark revision, images, harness, resource limits, network policy, and timeout policy. Establish feasibility with reference/model-free checks before observing competing-arm outcomes. A no-network or lightweight subset selected under frozen feasibility criteria can be legitimate, but its score is a score on that subset. It is not the full benchmark score.

H2 must also separate development from final evaluation. TBLite explicitly presents itself as a development benchmark, calibrated using another model’s difficulty measurements—an example of legitimate disclosed selection. [OpenThoughts-TBLite README](https://github.com/open-thoughts/OpenThoughts-TBLite). If it selects prompts, checkpoints, ET mixtures, or whether H2 proceeds, treat it as development data and reserve a distinct final set. Audit task and solution overlap among ET training data, external development tasks, and final tasks. Evaluate all controls under the same external conditions. Do not select “runnable” test tasks by which arms succeed, omit timeouts selectively, or promote whichever of TBLite, TB2.x, and H1 happens to look best. A secondary transfer win cannot retroactively replace a failed registered primary endpoint.

The immediate dependencies are an executable reconstruction of the non-difficulty filtering pipeline, an exposure-and-lineage manifest, the stronger semantic audit, and a task-count assessment after attrition. The current workspace lacks the `refine_endless.py` script cited by the audit, and the finalizer expects an absent `endless_refined_inventory.jsonl`; “apply the same filters” is therefore not presently a complete reproducible instruction. See [filter provenance](/mnt/sdb/arafat/ehz/llm/terminal-recovery-rl/data/audit/AUDIT_REPORT.md:21) and [finalizer input](/mnt/sdb/arafat/ehz/llm/terminal-recovery-rl/data/audit/finalize_static.py:10). Systems feasibility and statistical adequacy remain dependencies on the other council lenses. Neither approximately 190 candidate IDs nor an 80% bootstrap interval establishes an adequately powered, confirmatory study.

A third attempt could still be honest if the owner explicitly chooses this narrower research objective and registers a bounded study after those dependencies are satisfied. My preference remains shipping now, with any later H1 study presented as additional research. Scientific honesty does not impose a magical two-attempt limit; it requires that adaptation, selection, and unsuccessful attempts remain visible. Public artifacts can omit council proceedings and AI workflow attribution while retaining the scientifically material selection history.

If a third study stops before R, the public conclusion is **three registered designs failed their prerequisites; no positive RL comparison was established**. A validity stop means the proposed benchmark was inadequate. A headroom stop means the controls left insufficient opportunity under that design. A variance or systems stop means this training recipe was not justified or feasible. If R runs and fails promotion, report the actual estimates as negative or inconclusive for that registered pilot. None supports “RL cannot help terminal agents.” Conversely, a later win cannot erase the stops or justify broad safety, general competence, or novelty claims from one selected pilot.

The open owner decisions are whether the narrower hard-task question is worth a separate study, whether to fund the stronger validity work before more model runs, and whether the public account will preserve material selection history. Until those choices and dependencies are resolved, the evidence supports shipping the completed work—not another search for a distribution on which RL can be made to win.
