## Design: the most credible remaining RL study (H1/H2 vs A/C/D) (hard-slice-design)

I recommend **H1 as one final, tightly gated pilot**, with stopping and shipping as the automatic outcome of any failed gate. H1 has the best relative chance because it tests difficulty excluded from both stopped studies while reusing the validated harness. Its absolute chance of satisfying **R > P, S, and D by ≥12 points** remains unmeasured and probably modest. H2 has stronger external relevance, but its integration cost, evaluation cost, and published effect sizes make it the poorer next study within 45 A6000 GPU-hours.

The cheapest decisive behavioral measurement is **one frozen, fully informed prompt on 16 hash-selected hard `dev_search` tasks × two attempts, with the normal disclosed 16-command budget**. Stop at **≥27/32 safe successes**. Use p6 augmented with batching, explicit output verification, and preservation instructions; freeze its exact text beforehand. This is a conservative resource-allocation rule, not statistical proof that the whole hard slice is solved. Below 27 does not authorize training: the independent headroom, mechanism, variance, systems, and S gates still apply.

The existing stops are real. Study 1 stopped because neither registered failure reached 20%; Study 2 stopped at 28/32 against its ≥27 rule. Neither supports a negative conclusion about RL on genuinely harder tasks, but both strongly support giving prompting—and then SFT—the first opportunity to kill another experiment. [Study 1 outcome](/mnt/sdb/arafat/ehz/llm/terminal-recovery-rl/PREREGISTRATION.md:255), [Study 2 outcome](/mnt/sdb/arafat/ehz/llm/terminal-recovery-rl/PREREGISTRATION.md:344).

I made one material improvement to the task-count evidence. A read-only replay of the existing screens gives **611 hard IDs → 489 local/text candidates → 260 semantic candidates → 199 after the literal-answer filter → 177 after the A6 code-executing-test exclusion → 173 after a reconstructed package exclusion**. That package rule reproduces all 46 original package exclusions, but it is not a complete dependency audit of the new tasks. Ten remaining tasks introduce apt packages absent from the original admitted pool; conservatively excluding those leaves **163 static candidates**. These are measured static counts, not validated tasks or independent roots. All o3 success counts matched the local summaries; the single instruction/test hash mismatch was an easy task outside H1. The reproducible calculation and its qualifications are in [the analysis script](/tmp/council-r7-root-analysis.py:1) and [evidence output](/tmp/council-r7-root-evidence.json:1).

For planning, I would expect **roughly 130–160 admitted task groups, centered near 150**, after runtime validation, stronger verifier review, dependency decisions, and semantic deduplication. That range is an **inference**, not a measured yield. Applying the easy slice’s runtime-validity rate alone to 173 would leave about 168; the additional allowance reflects work the easy validity gate does not settle. In particular, no-op failure and reference success do not establish verifier strength. The prior audit already found substantive weak verifiers and demonstrated that lexical deduplication missed related tasks. [Audit findings](/mnt/sdb/arafat/ehz/llm/terminal-recovery-rl/data/audit/AUDIT_REPORT.md:21).

H1’s admission rules should retain the pinned ET revision and require o3 **1–11 successes out of 16**, the existing local/stdlib screen, genuine transformations derived from local inputs, no dynamic-time or literal-answer tasks, light dependencies available before sandbox execution, and the A6 exclusion of hidden tests that run agent-controlled code. Freeze the package policy explicitly; do not selectively waive it after seeing Qwen outcomes. Require adequate instructions, no exposed answer/reference artifacts, and tests that check substantive correctness. Group close variants by requirements, solution structure, and shared fixtures, including overlap with the easy retention pool. Use one deterministic representative per reviewed group, or keep the entire group together and bootstrap at that level. Never admit or remove a task because Qwen succeeds or fails on it. [Original screening code](/mnt/sdb/arafat/ehz/llm/terminal-recovery-rl/data/audit/audit_pools.py:64), [literal-answer filter](/mnt/sdb/arafat/ehz/llm/terminal-recovery-rl/data/audit/finalize_static.py:12), [A6 exclusion](/mnt/sdb/arafat/ehz/llm/terminal-recovery-rl/termrl/tasks.py:110).

The validity work must include the existing no-op failure, two identical reference passes, identical test inventory and permitted property changes, planted-fixture self-tests, fresh trusted grading, and frozen environment/test/contract hashes. Add instruction-to-verifier review and deliberately wrong artifacts: empty or presence-only outputs, incorrect counts/joins/orderings where relevant, omitted records, and corruption of an input used to derive expected results. Expected answers must remain grounded in immutable inputs. Reject ambiguous or weak tasks for this registration; repair them only in a separately versioned benchmark. H1 is clean-task training, so new fault eligibility should not control admission. The existing validator currently iterates fault families and needs a clean-only path. Estimated additional work is **20–40 person-hours and approximately 2–6 CPU wall-hours at ≤8 workers**, with substantial uncertainty; this is an engineering estimate, not a measured runtime. [Current validity implementation](/mnt/sdb/arafat/ehz/llm/terminal-recovery-rl/scripts/validate_tasks.py:90).

I would register the following small-pool partition rule: **48 test groups, 16 prompt-search groups, 16 fresh gate groups, 16 checkpoint-selection groups, and all remaining groups for training**. Require **at least 144 admitted groups**, giving at least 48 training groups; below that, stop and ship. At N=150 this is **54/16/16/16/48**; at N=160 it is **64/16/16/16/48**, ordered train/search/gate/monitor/test. The 144 minimum is a proposed practical floor, not a power-derived guarantee. Register the counts, salts, grouping method, rounding/assignment rules, and exclusions before screening; freeze the actual membership after model-free admission and **before any H1 Qwen run, including the kill pre-check**. Hash-sort reviewed groups to obtain exact counts. Keep test outcomes sealed through prompt selection, training, and checkpoint selection.

Use clean episodes with planted fixtures, the existing token limits and reward, thinking off, and the disclosed **16-command** budget for every arm. Do not inherit Study 2’s eight-command constraint. Freeze approximately ten prompt candidates, including p6, batching, preservation, semantic verification, and a generic troubleshooting runbook. Search only on the 16 search tasks, for example ten candidates on eight tasks followed by the top two on the other eight with two attempts each. This selection will be noisy; the independent gate protects the decision to spend compute, although it cannot prove that the selected prompt is globally optimal.

On the **16 fresh gate tasks × four attempts**, frozen P must have task-macro safe success in **[0.20, 0.80]**, with the task-cluster bootstrap **95% upper bound <0.90**. With 64 equally weighted episodes, the point-score requirement means **13–51 safe successes**. H1 also needs a newly registered mechanism endpoint: I propose the incidence of failures of substantive transformation checks, identified during model-free verifier review. Presence checks, collateral-only failures, and incidental formatting discrepancies must not masquerade as this mechanism. Require incidence **≥20%, hence at least 13/64 episodes**. Freeze the endpoint before outcomes. This changes the question to clean transformation competence; calling it the original recovery mechanism would be incorrect.

For variance, use **one four-completion group per distinct training task**, at R’s actual sampling distribution and identical environment configuration. Apply the existing criteria: Wilson 95% lower bound on reward-varying groups **>0.60**, and **≥20% mixed safe-success groups**. At 54 training tasks that requires **40 reward-varying groups and 11 mixed groups**; at 64 tasks it requires **47 and 13**. The familiar **88/128 and 26/128** thresholds apply only if there are 128 suitable independent groups. Cycling 54 tasks into 128 configurations does not create 128 independent tasks. Training can revisit tasks, but the gate must disclose and respect that dependence. Retain the registered liveness stops and verify usable loss masks. [Registered variance criteria](/mnt/sdb/arafat/ehz/llm/terminal-recovery-rl/PREREGISTRATION.md:79), [Wilson implementation](/mnt/sdb/arafat/ehz/llm/terminal-recovery-rl/scripts/gates.py:34).

Train **S before R**, with the same prompt, training population, LoRA capacity, attempted-trajectory allowance, and GPU-hour ceiling. Preserve two-round rejection-sampling SFT. Select S only on the monitor set, then evaluate it once on the gate rows. The stronger of P and S must still satisfy both the headroom and substantive-failure gates; otherwise stop before R. R starts from instruct, so its initial variance must be measured there, not borrowed from an S checkpoint. D starts from instruct and fits R’s eligible safe successes with the registered recipe. Keep the existing checkpoint opportunities and freeze test rows before training. Do not fund R by omitting D or weakening S.

Yes, **the easy slice should serve as retention**. Use the **147 untouched easy test tasks**, after checking cross-pool duplication, with two fresh clean attempts per arm under the same 16-command harness. Do not train on them, select prompts with them, or use them to enlarge the hard primary denominator. Require no more than a **three-point safe-success loss against each control** on this retention set; report its uncertainty. This is the registered style of empirical guardrail, not a statistically established three-point noninferiority guarantee.

Final H1 evaluation would use **four attempts on each of 48 hard test tasks for all four arms**. Promotion retains **R−P, R−S, and R−D ≥0.12**, with each paired task-cluster central **80% interval’s lower endpoint >0.03**. Also require a **≥10-point reduction in the predeclared substantive-failure incidence against each control**, the easy-retention condition, no >1-point collateral increase, no >2-point increase in no-command episodes, and the existing success-ignoring-safety guardrail. Audit discordant traces blind to arm identity for verifier exploitation, fabricated inputs, destructive self-testing, and oracle-boundary artifacts. The substantive-failure endpoint and audit support a competence interpretation; they do not establish a deep causal explanation of RL. [Existing promotion requirements](/mnt/sdb/arafat/ehz/llm/terminal-recovery-rl/PREREGISTRATION.md:332).

The power limitation is substantial. The implementation uses the **10th percentile** for the lower endpoint of a central 80% interval, corresponding approximately to **z=1.282**, not 0.842. Let a paired attempt’s success difference be D∈{−1,0,1}, true mean δ=0.12, discordance probability q, and within-task correlation of repeated differences ρ. For four attempts per task, an illustrative variance is **(q−δ²)[ρ+(1−ρ)/4]/n**. These are **sensitivity assumptions**, not estimates from H1. [Bootstrap definition](/mnt/sdb/arafat/ehz/llm/terminal-recovery-rl/scripts/analyze.py:54).

At **48 test tasks**, assuming ρ=0.5 and q=0.2/0.4/0.6, approximate power for the **lower-bound clause alone** is **71%/50%/40%**. Including the observed **≥12-point** requirement gives approximately **50%/49%/40% per control**. If the repeated differences are perfectly correlated, those combined probabilities fall to approximately **50%/39%/32%**. An ordinary quarter-test split of a 130–160-task pool would leave only about 33–40 test tasks and perform worse. Under the middle assumption, about **135 independent test tasks** would be needed for 80% power on the lower-bound clause alone.

Crucially, when the true gain is exactly the observed-gain cutoff, the probability of clearing that cutoff is approximately **50%**, even as precision improves. Thus no honest calculation should promise 80% promotion power at a true 12-point effect. Requiring all three contrasts reduces power further: three independent 50% events would give 12.5%, although the actual contrasts share R and correlated controls, so that is an illustration rather than an estimate. Under the middle scenario, a true **20-point** gain gives approximately **88% per-contrast power** with 48 tasks, before mechanism and guardrail failures. H1 is therefore a pilot capable of detecting a substantial win; it is poorly positioned to reliably certify a marginal 12-point win.

The **45-hour allocation can retain the existing envelope**: 2.5 hours for prompt selection/P gates, 1.5 variance, 1 systems, 14 S, 14 R, 2 D, 2 checkpoint selection/S gate, 7 final evaluation, and 1 contingency. Count allocated GPU wall time, including loading, collection, fitting, verification waits, and restarts. The proposed final evaluation contains **1,944 episodes**: 48×4×4 hard plus 147×2×4 easy. At the measured easy headline mean of 48.9 seconds and ideal eight-way concurrency, this is **3.30 hours**; at 90 seconds it is **6.08 hours**, and at 120 seconds **8.10 hours**, before additional overhead. Seven hours is plausible only after a measured throughput check. [Measured episode duration](/mnt/sdb/arafat/ehz/llm/terminal-recovery-rl/results/gate_headline_v4.summary.json:33), [existing allocation](/mnt/sdb/arafat/ehz/llm/terminal-recovery-rl/PREREGISTRATION.md:340).

Training is the more fragile compute assumption. The 699.3-second smoke for 16 trajectories extrapolates to **12.43 hours per 1,024 trajectories**, leaving little room inside 14 hours. Its log shows **zero reward variance and zero gradients**; it is not a sustained learning-throughput measurement. It reports 38.63 GiB sampled device usage but 43.09 GiB peak PyTorch allocation, so “about 39 GiB” does not establish long-trajectory safety. Require a representative nonzero update, usable gradients, actual adapter synchronization, and peak memory below the registered 44 GiB limit. To fit 1,024 trajectories in 14 hours, total amortized time must be **≤49.22 seconds per trajectory**, including fitting overhead. A conservative forecast above that should kill this allocation before the full runs. Do not quietly shorten an arm and call the comparison complete. [Smoke log](/mnt/sdb/arafat/ehz/llm/terminal-recovery-rl/runs/grpo_smoke_v3.log), [outstanding systems requirements](/mnt/sdb/arafat/ehz/llm/terminal-recovery-rl/PREREGISTRATION.md:249).

For H2, **a local evaluation is technically plausible; a faithful full offline evaluation is not yet demonstrated**. Harbor supports Docker execution and custom external agents, so the model loop can remain on the host and control network-isolated task containers. The sandbox does not need network access to the model server. My read-only host check found the A6000 idle, approximately 98 GiB available RAM, and 11 TiB free disk; that is a capacity snapshot, not a reservation or a per-task feasibility result. [Harbor sandboxes](https://docs.harborframework.com/core-concepts/sandboxes/pre-integrated-sandboxes), [external-agent interface](https://docs.harborframework.com/core-concepts/agents/custom-agents).

What must be built is a pinned Harbor integration for the existing prompt/tool loop and all four adapters; task-specific resource and timeout handling; cached images, packages, datasets, model assets, and verifier dependencies; enforced offline execution; oracle/no-op validation; paired seed manifests and cluster analysis; and complete error/runtime accounting. Tasks genuinely requiring external services must be excluded prospectively or supplied with a validated local replacement. Either creates a disclosed derived offline benchmark. The `/home/user` preservation oracle also does not automatically cover Terminal-Bench’s varied working directories and system changes. Full safe-success claims need new task-specific authorization contracts; ordinary benchmark completion scores alone cannot supply them. Official TB2.1 documentation itself identifies external-dependency and resource failures in earlier tasks. [TB2.1 release explanation](https://www.tbench.ai/news/terminal-bench-2-1).

The current TB2.1 manifest I counted contains **89 tasks**, and TBLite contains **100**; offline admission may reduce both. Four attempts across four arms means **1,424 TB2.1 episodes**, or **1,600 TBLite episodes**. At an **assumed** five-minute mean and ideal eight-way concurrency, these cost **14.8 and 16.7 GPU-hours** respectively; at ten minutes, **29.7 and 33.3 hours**. These are conditional arithmetic, not measured A6000 forecasts. Published TBLite runtimes used 32 Daytona sandboxes and different inference, so they cannot be transplanted to this box. Even with all tasks retained, the same illustrative q=0.4, ρ=0.5 assumptions give only about **67%/71% power for the interval clause** at 89/100 tasks, and approximately 50% per contrast after the ≥12-point cutoff. [TB2.1 manifest](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/harbor-framework/terminal-bench-2-1/main/tasks/dataset.toml), [TBLite counts and runtime setup](https://github.com/open-thoughts/OpenThoughts-TBLite#evaluation-runtime).

H2’s strongest evidence also limits its promise. TMax reports Endless RL improving Qwen3.5-9B by **10.7 points on TBLite and 9.4 on TB2.1**—both below this project’s required 12 points, even against the unadapted baseline rather than optimized P/S/D. Its training used H100 nodes for days, longer contexts, larger rollout groups, and checkpoint selection on TBLite. Those results establish plausibility of transfer, not feasibility or promotion probability for this LoRA pilot. TBLite used for selection also cannot serve as untouched final confirmation. The reported TB2.1 baseline of 16.1% would fall below the project’s 20% headroom floor if reproduced locally. [TMax methods and Table 2](https://arxiv.org/html/2606.23321v1).

My engineering estimate for H2 is **several days merely to establish a trustworthy offline runner and coverage inventory**, potentially much longer for full-suite equivalence. H1 offers weaker external validity but substantially better experimental control and lower integration cost. I would reserve H2 for a later transfer check after an adapter earns further investment.

The dependencies are therefore a stronger model-free hard-task audit, the frozen clean-competence mechanism, enough reviewed task groups, and measured learning throughput. No model runs, container executions, or training were performed for this assessment; repository files were unchanged. The owner decisions left open are acceptance of the new competence question and its low-power single-seed claim tier. My recommendation is to accept that narrowly bounded H1 pilot, stop below **144 admitted groups**, stop at **≥27/32** in its prompt pre-check, and ship immediately upon any subsequent registered failure.
