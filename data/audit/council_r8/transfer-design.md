## Design: an honest ET-to-Terminal-Bench transfer study on one A6000 (transfer-design)

A credible **conditional pilot** fits this box. I would register Study 4 as transfer from a fixed mixture of validated ET easy and admitted ET hard tasks to a sealed, offline-admissible TBLite sample. I would omit TB2.1, give S and R 768 training attempts each, and reserve enough compute to finish all four controls. This is not ready for training today: admission, a working TB evaluator, and measured long-episode throughput remain prerequisites. Failure of any prerequisite means stop and ship the frozen prompted agent with the three existing stops reported honestly.

The claim would be narrow: “ET outcome RL improved complete task success on this independently held-out TBLite subset beyond P, S, and D.” It would not establish official TBLite performance, TB2.1 performance, or general terminal safety.

Verified evidence changes several details of the proposal. The 699.3-second GRPO smoke recorded **zero reward variance and zero gradients**; its approximately 893- and 761-token mean completions also did not exercise the full context limit. It provides a timing anchor, not a successful learning or worst-case memory gate. Conversely, the 32K inference server did initialize with 549,457 cached tokens and an advertised 16.77-request capacity at 32,768 tokens. That supports testing eight concurrent evaluations, but does not establish their throughput. See [GRPO smoke](/mnt/sdb/arafat/ehz/llm/terminal-recovery-rl/runs/grpo_smoke_v3/run_summary.json:2) and [32K server log](/mnt/sdb/arafat/ehz/llm/terminal-recovery-rl/runs/vllm_32k_probe.log:56).

My read-only inventory reproduced 100 TBLite tasks, 44 graders without installation/network commands, and 89 TB2.1 tasks whose graders require network preparation. **Forty of those 44 TBLite tasks have one-hour agent timeouts.** Furthermore, 40 TBLite wrappers deliberately pass pytest regardless of the underlying task score. The evaluator must consume the native reward, not pytest’s exit status. Some graders query running localhost services, so the ET snapshot-and-fresh-container grader cannot be substituted unchanged. Concrete examples are [the scoring wrapper](/mnt/sdb/arafat/ehz/llm/.pools/tblite/multi-server-configuration/tests/test_outputs.py:24) and [its live-service grader](/mnt/sdb/arafat/ehz/llm/.pools/tblite/multi-server-configuration/tests/grader.py:70).

Two other corrections matter. TBLite’s pinned [README declares Apache 2.0](/mnt/sdb/arafat/ehz/llm/.pools/tblite/README.md:113), despite lacking a standalone licence file. And the hard ET pool is **at most 103 candidates**, not 103 admitted tasks. At inspection, it contained 25 validity records and 20 draft contracts, with no finalized hard contract file. The [Study 3 stop](/mnt/sdb/arafat/ehz/llm/terminal-recovery-rl/PREREGISTRATION.md:449) remains binding.

I would train on **both ET strata, with 75% easy and 25% admitted hard configurations**, all clean with their validated preservation fixtures. Freeze 192 distinct training task groups: 144 easy and 48 hard. Each supplies four attempts, giving 768 trajectories. Reserve a separate ET checkpoint set of 12 easy and four hard groups. Thus the design needs at least 52 genuinely admitted hard groups, after removing duplicates and relatives of retained evaluation tasks. Complete the existing semantic review, reference/no-op checks, mutation checks, and contract freezing first. Never reinstate the 68 rejected tasks. Preserve the existing easy final split; exclude hard relatives of those tasks from training. If this training population cannot be assembled, stop this design rather than quietly switching distributions.

This mixture is a judgment, not an empirical finding: easy tasks provide reliable supervision, while the smaller hard stratum broadens the training signal without letting a small, suspect tail dominate. Use no injected faults and no eight-command restriction. Those would recycle objectives that already stopped. Keep the existing safety-aware outcome reward, with substantive versus superficial checks annotated for the variance gate. Freeze the mixture before observing any new Qwen outcomes; do not reweight toward whichever stratum happens to produce variance.

For the external suite, audit **all 100 pinned TBLite tasks**, admitting both naturally offline graders and graders that become offline through dependency staging alone. Restricting admission to the initial 44 would leave too few independent final tasks after development allocation and would disproportionately select the longest timeout class. Downloading image layers and preparing pinned verifier dependencies during trusted setup is allowed. Agent execution and grading have no external network. Localhost services remain available. Grader dependencies must be unavailable to the agent until grading; installing them into the agent’s environment could inadvertently make tasks easier.

Admission is entirely model-free. Require reproducible builds; preserved instruction and test semantics; a reference that earns full credit twice under the registered resources; a no-op that fails; meaningful wrong-output mutations that fail; no exposed answer; and a consistent instruction–verifier contract. Dependency staging may replace download/install plumbing, but may not change expected outputs, grading criteria, or the task’s installation challenge. Exclude tasks genuinely requiring external services or online agent downloads. Audit overlapping instructions, fixtures, solutions, and task lineage against ET and within TBLite. Preserve native working directories, users, and service lifecycle. Use declared CPU/RAM allocations; where absent, register two CPUs and 4 GiB explicitly as this study’s resource setting.

Require **80 independent admitted external task groups**. Choose one representative per group by a frozen rule, then assign 16 to prompt development, 16 to the headroom gate, and 48 to final evaluation. Use one preregistered seed and a deterministic assignment balancing category and timeout strata. Freeze task IDs, exclusions, images, grader dependencies, and split hashes before any Qwen external episode. Surplus admitted groups remain unused. Fewer than 80 means stop; the number must not shrink after seeing attrition.

The final 48 groups receive **two independent attempts per arm**. They remain unavailable for prompting, training, checkpoint selection, runtime experiments, and debugging with Qwen. Model-free admission necessarily inspects their specifications and verifiers; that is allowed, but none of those materials or reference solutions enter policy training or task-specific prompts. All checkpoint selection occurs on ET. TBLite development outcomes may select P and determine whether to proceed, but may not select R’s checkpoint. This sacrifices some optimization opportunity in exchange for a cleaner transfer claim.

I would run **no TB2.1 episodes in Study 4**. Its network-enabled specification, verifier preparation, heterogeneous workloads, and additional four-arm evaluation do not fit this budget comfortably. Disabling its network and reporting the result as ordinary TB2.1 would also misstate the evaluation. TB2.1 belongs in a separately funded follow-up after a positive pilot, with its own frozen network policy.

The external harness would use **32,768 total context tokens, 16,384 cumulative assistant-generated tokens, 64 bash invocations, a 300-second per-command timeout, and each task’s original agent wall-clock limit**. Each command also stops at the remaining episode deadline. Use 6,000-character head/tail tool output, persistent bash, thinking disabled, and the existing evaluation decoding. No rolling summaries or silent context eviction. Stop and grade the current state on any exhausted limit. Preserve each task’s verifier timeout separately. The twelve-minute figure below is a throughput assumption, **not an episode timeout**.

Implement a TB-specific environment adapter with the benchmark’s grading lifecycle. In particular, preserve live services through grading; do not force `/home/user`, recursively change ownership, or apply the ET preservation oracle to arbitrary `/app` tasks. Define the primary as binary full credit according to the frozen native reward contract, with native partial reward reported separately. Protect and validate grading inputs and outputs, reject malformed rewards, and audit possible tampering. A passing wrapper test is insufficient.

Training remains at **16 commands, 30 seconds per command, 8,192 total tokens, and the existing 6,144-token completion allowance**. That allowance currently includes tool-result tokens, despite the shared context’s shorthand description as generated tokens; see [the actual configuration](/mnt/sdb/arafat/ehz/llm/terminal-recovery-rl/termrl/config.py:39). Keep that behavior identical for S collection and R training. The external evaluator needs separate counters for generated tokens and total transcript length.

The first behavioral gate is the familiar **kill pre-check: 16 external prompt-development tasks × two attempts with one frozen, fully informed generic prompt; stop at ≥27/32 full successes**. If it survives, compare four frozen prompt candidates on eight development tasks once each, then the best two on the other eight twice each. Include the informed prompt, batching, specification checking, and verification guidance without task-specific answers. Select by task-macro full success, breaking ties by fewer generated tokens. The selected prompt serves every arm.

Next, evaluate P on the 16 separate external gate tasks × four attempts. Require task-macro full success in **[20%, 80%]**, a task-cluster bootstrap 95% upper bound **below 90%**, and **at least 20% substantive task failures**. Define substantive requirements during admission, before model runs; format-only failures do not establish the desired opportunity. After selecting S, run it on the same gate configurations with the registered paired seeds. The stronger of P and S must still satisfy these conditions before R starts. A low external baseline is not automatically useful headroom: below 20%, this small training experiment stops.

The variance gate uses 128 distinct training groups—96 easy and 32 hard—with four attempts on the identical configuration at training decoding. Require **at least 88/128 groups with differing semantic outcomes**, giving the registered Wilson 95% lower bound above 60%, and **at least 26/128 groups containing both safe complete successes and failures**. Differences solely in formatting, fixture damage, or infrastructure errors cannot satisfy the semantic criterion. These diagnostic attempts do not become an extra S training corpus. A failure stops the study; it does not authorize increasing group size or searching a new mixture.

The systems gate must demonstrate actual learning: several representative GRPO updates with nonzero advantages, finite nonzero gradients, changed adapter weights, and verified adapter synchronization; device memory **below 44 GiB**; and successful eight-stream long-context inference with the adapter. Exercise near-limit sequences explicitly. Require zero unresolved systematic grading defects, an external infrastructure-error rate no higher than 1%, and no co-tenant disruption. Count all containers, including verifier containers, toward eight. Never restart dockerd.

The systems gate also needs two measured timing conditions. First, representative steady training must project 768 trajectories within ten hours **including 15% overhead**, which means at most approximately **40.8 amortized seconds per trajectory before that overhead**. Second, external development runs must support an effective twelve-minute episode lifecycle at eight-way concurrency after an overhead allowance, including grading, scheduling gaps, and resource contention. Measure elapsed batch time; do not assume that eight nominal workers produce eight effective workers. The existing 532 tokens/second measurement at 16 streams cannot justify an eight-sandbox budget.

The arms retain their intended attribution. **P** is frozen instruct plus the selected prompt. **S** receives 384 attempts in each of two rejection-sampling rounds; round two uses the fixed round-one epoch-two actor, and its final SFT fit starts from instruct on the union of safe successes. **R** starts from instruct with LoRA r16, Dr.GRPO, group size four, four groups per update, β=0, no standard-deviation scaling, and the existing learning rate and importance-sampling correction: 48 updates ×16 trajectories =768 attempts. **D** starts from instruct and fits all eligible safe successes collected by R, using the same SFT recipe and assistant-token masking.

S and R have **identical 768-attempt and ten-GPU-hour ceilings**, the same training configurations, and the same rollout limits. Both must complete their registered work; an incomplete control cannot be discarded. S, R, and D each receive two checkpoint-selection opportunities: S’s final-round epochs one/two, R’s updates 24/48, and D’s epochs one/two, evaluated on the ET checkpoint set × two attempts. D gets two fitting hours and must complete its prescribed fit; otherwise the comparison is incomplete. D’s collection is shared with R and is not charged twice. These are equal data-acquisition budgets for S/R and equal final inference budgets for all arms—not a claim that P or D consumes the same training compute.

The budget is **45 A6000 GPU-hours**, reserving: S 10; R 10; D 2; systems and startup probes 2; variance 1; ET checkpoint selection and retention 2.5; external development 5.6; external final evaluation 9.6; contingency 2.3. Model initialization, failed attempts, restarts, grading waits while the card is reserved, and saving count. CPU-only admission and image preparation are separate elapsed work, potentially several days.

The arithmetic is explicit. ET collection at the measured 49 seconds per episode gives **768 ×(49/60) ÷8 ÷60 =1.31 hours** for S’s collection, before fitting. The GRPO timing anchor gives **768 ×(699.3/16) ÷3,600 =9.32 hours**; this already includes rollout/update execution and must **not** be divided by eight again. Its narrow margin is why the fresh nonzero-gradient throughput gate is mandatory.

External development comprises 32 kill attempts, 64 prompt-selection attempts, 64 P gate attempts, and 64 S gate attempts: **224 ×12 minutes ÷8 ÷60 =5.6 hours**. Final evaluation is **48 tasks ×2 attempts ×4 arms =384 episodes**, costing **384 ×12 ÷8 ÷60 =9.6 hours**. Variance costs **512 ×(49/60) ÷8 ÷60 =0.87 hours**. ET checkpoint selection costs approximately 0.33 hours, and the previously registered 147-task retention panel ×two attempts ×four arms costs approximately 2.00 hours. Verify that exact retention manifest before registration.

This budget fails visibly if the timing assumptions fail. At four effective external workers, external evaluation alone takes **30.4 hours**, bringing the plan above 57 hours before contingency. At eight workers but twenty-minute episodes, it takes **25.33 hours**, bringing the plan above 52 hours before contingency. Do not rescue either case by shortening task deadlines, dropping D, reducing the final split after inspection, or evaluating only R’s favorable tasks. Stop before training when the projection fails; report incomplete if an unexpected later overrun prevents completion.

Promotion retains the owner’s threshold: R must exceed **each of P, S, and D by ≥12 percentage points**, with every paired task-cluster bootstrap **central 80% interval’s lower endpoint >+3 points**. Resample task/lineage groups with their paired attempts intact, never individual trajectories as independent tasks. Require a **≥10-point reduction in substantive failure** against each control, plus a blinded audit of all discordant task outcomes and sampled shared outcomes that finds no verifier exploitation or invalid solution. This supports a substantive-competence interpretation; it does not establish fault-recovery learning.

For product guardrails, retain the ET preservation oracle on the frozen ET retention panel. Against every control, permit at most **three points of safe-success loss, one point of increased collateral damage, and two points of increased no-command behavior**. Audit external violations of explicit preservation requirements, but do not describe unmeasured TB collateral as zero. A discovered grader defect requires symmetric handling across arms and may invalidate the study. Passing this rule promotes the research result, not unrestricted deployment.

Short ET training and long TB evaluation do not invalidate the comparison. The intervention is the training algorithm; all arms face the same external distribution and inference allowances. The horizon change is part of the transfer being tested. **R should not receive longer training horizons merely because TB permits them.** Longer allowances do not create useful long-horizon experience when ET tasks finish quickly, and longer backpropagation threatens this card’s memory and throughput. If legitimate ET tasks repeatedly truncate, stop under the registered liveness rule; changing horizons would require a new common training configuration for S and R. Retain the existing stops for three gradient-free updates, nonfinite values, insufficient informative groups, and excessive truncation.

The remaining dependencies are concrete: finish hard-task admission, admit 80 external groups, implement and verify native TB scoring and service preservation, freeze the manifests, update scripts currently hardcoded for 1,024 attempts, and demonstrate the two throughput conditions. I performed no new model episodes, training, or container workloads for this assessment.

The principal risk is a low chance of promotion, even if everything fits. Forty-eight final task groups and two repeats remain a small, single-training-seed pilot. TMax establishes plausibility, but its ET result was +10.7 TBLite points over the base model, with substantially larger training resources and TBLite checkpoint selection; that does not predict +12 over optimized P, S, and D here. [TMax methods and results](https://arxiv.org/html/2606.23321v1#S4).

I assume the owner accepts this restricted offline-transfer claim and the reduced 768-attempt training budget. Acceptance of those limits is the remaining owner decision; no response is needed for this council assessment. If the owner requires full Terminal-Bench performance, a general safety claim, or a reasonably powered confirmation within the same 45 hours, **no credible version fits**. The appropriate action is to ship the prompted agent and stopped-study artifacts, or fund a separately registered experiment with dedicated evaluation capacity—not weaken the controls or manufacture headroom.
